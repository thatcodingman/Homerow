About the 2 minute typing test

A two-minute test shows how well you hold your speed once the early burst wears off. It rewards a steady rhythm and good posture over raw sprinting, which makes it a better picture of how you type during real work. You get your WPM, accuracy, raw WPM, errors, and consistency, plus a keyboard heatmap of the keys you missed.

What is a good score on a 2 minute typing test?

  • Under 30 WPM — beginner, still building muscle memory for key positions
  • 35–45 WPM — average for most adults doing everyday typing
  • 50–65 WPM — solid, comfortable touch-typing speed
  • 65–90 WPM — fast, well above the office-work average
  • 100+ WPM — professional transcriptionist or competitive-typist territory

Many people score a little lower here than on a 30 second test, because fatigue and focus come into play over a longer run. That is normal.

Tips for the full two minutes

  • Start at a pace you can hold, not your top speed
  • Keep your shoulders and wrists relaxed; tension builds up over two minutes
  • Watch your consistency score: a steady rhythm usually means fewer errors and a higher sustainable WPM
  • If your accuracy drops late in the test, slow down slightly instead of pushing through

Try a different length

Want something shorter or longer? Try the 30 second typing test, 1 minute typing test, 3 minute typing test, 5 minute typing test. For tips on raising your score, read how to type faster.

2 Minute typing test FAQ

Why is my 2 minute WPM lower than my 30 second WPM?

Sustained typing is harder than a short burst. Fatigue, lapses in focus, and a slightly cautious pace all lower your average over two minutes, so a small drop is normal.

How is the 2 minute test scored?

Your WPM is your correctly typed characters divided by 5, then divided by 2 minutes. The FAQ has the full explanation, including raw WPM and consistency.

Will I run out of text?

No. The passage is long enough to keep going for the whole two minutes, even at very high typing speeds.
