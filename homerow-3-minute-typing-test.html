About the 3 minute typing test

A three-minute test asks for focus as well as speed. Early bursts matter less and a steady rhythm matters more, which makes it a good stand-in for real typing work like emails, notes, and reports. You get your WPM, accuracy, raw WPM, errors, and consistency, plus a keyboard heatmap of the keys you missed.

What is a good score on a 3 minute typing test?

  • Under 30 WPM — beginner, still building muscle memory for key positions
  • 35–45 WPM — average for most adults doing everyday typing
  • 50–65 WPM — solid, comfortable touch-typing speed
  • 65–90 WPM — fast, well above the office-work average
  • 100+ WPM — professional transcriptionist or competitive-typist territory

Expect a slightly lower number than a one-minute test; holding pace for three minutes is harder than sprinting for sixty seconds.

Tips for three steady minutes

  • Pick a pace you can hold for the whole test rather than starting fast and fading
  • Relax your shoulders, wrists, and fingers; unnecessary tension slows you down over time
  • Keep your eyes on the text and trust your fingers to find the keys
  • If you notice mistakes piling up, ease off a little, because clean typing recovers speed faster than rushing

Try a different length

Want something shorter or longer? Try the 30 second typing test, 1 minute typing test, 2 minute typing test, 5 minute typing test. For tips on raising your score, read how to type faster.

3 Minute typing test FAQ

Why is my 3 minute score lower than my 1 minute score?

Sustained typing is harder than a short run. Fatigue and small lapses in focus lower your average over a longer test, so a modest drop is normal.

How is the 3 minute test scored?

Your WPM is your correctly typed characters divided by 5, then divided by 3 minutes. The FAQ covers raw WPM, accuracy, and consistency.

Will I run out of text?

No. The passage is long enough to last all three minutes, even at very high typing speeds.
