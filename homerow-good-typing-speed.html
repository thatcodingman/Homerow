What is a good typing speed?

WPM benchmarks and what they mean

The short answer: around 40 WPM is average, 60 or more is good, 80 or more is fast, and 100+ is exceptional. What counts as "good" for you depends on what you type and how accurately. Want your own number first? Take the typing test.

Typing speed benchmarks

  • Under 30 WPM — beginner, still building muscle memory for key positions
  • 35–45 WPM — average for most adults doing everyday typing
  • 50–65 WPM — solid, comfortable touch-typing speed
  • 65–90 WPM — fast, well above the office-work average
  • 100+ WPM — professional transcriptionist or competitive-typist territory

How WPM is measured

Typing tests count one "word" as 5 characters, spaces included, so a speed of 60 WPM means 300 correct characters per minute. Net WPM only counts what you typed correctly; raw WPM counts every keystroke. The exact formulas are in the FAQ.

Does accuracy matter as much as speed?

Yes, and usually more. A common rule of thumb is to aim for 95% accuracy or higher, because every error costs a correction and breaks your rhythm. Someone typing 55 WPM cleanly will often finish real work faster than someone typing 70 WPM with constant backspacing. Our guide to improving typing accuracy covers how to tighten this up.

How fast do you need to type?

  • Everyday use (messages, email, browsing): 30-40 WPM is plenty
  • Office and administrative work: roughly 40-60 WPM is a comfortable range
  • Data entry and transcription: job listings often ask for 60 WPM or more, though requirements vary by employer
  • Programming and writing: typing speed matters less than accuracy, because most of the time goes to thinking, not typing
  • Competitive typing: 100+ WPM is common among serious competitors

Why your score changes from test to test

Your WPM is not one fixed number. Test length, mode, and the text itself all move it. Punctuation and Quotes modes are usually a little slower than plain Words mode because of the extra Shift and pinky reaches. Short 15-second tests reward a burst of speed, while 60-second and longer tests show your sustainable pace. Warming up with a test or two also tends to give a better score than a cold start, so judge yourself by your average over several tests rather than a single run.

How to get faster

Most people improve quickly once they fix the basics: fingers on the home row, eyes on the text, accuracy first. Our guide on how to type faster walks through nine habits that raise WPM, and the heatmap on your results screen shows which keys to practice.
