Frequently asked questions

WPM, accuracy, and how the test works

Quick answers about how Home Row scores your typing. Ready to see your own numbers? Take the typing test.

How is WPM actually calculated?

One "word" is standardized as 5 characters, including spaces — this is the industry-standard convention used by most typing tests, not a literal word count. Your WPM is your number of correctly-typed characters divided by 5, divided by the minutes elapsed.

What's the difference between WPM and Raw WPM?

WPM (the big number) only counts characters you typed correctly. Raw WPM counts everything you typed, mistakes included. If your Raw WPM is much higher than your WPM, you're typing fast but making errors that are dragging your real score down.

What's a good WPM?

Average typists land around 40 WPM (most adults fall between roughly 35 and 45). 60-70 WPM is solidly above average. Professional typists and competitive typing test regulars often break 100 WPM, though accuracy usually matters more than raw speed for real-world typing. Does backspacing to fix a mistake hurt my score?

No — once you've corrected a character, it's scored as correct. There's no penalty baked in for fixing a typo, only for leaving it wrong.

Why did my test end before the timer hit zero?

If you finish typing the entire passage before time runs out, the test ends immediately and scores what you typed — you don't have to wait out the clock once there's nothing left to type.

What do the Words, Quotes, and Punctuation modes do?

Words mode is the baseline — a plain stream of words for measuring raw speed. Quotes and Punctuation modes bring in the capital letters, punctuation marks, and sentence rhythm of real writing, which usually lowers your WPM a little because of the extra Shift and pinky reaches.

What is the heatmap on my results screen?

The "Where you slipped" keyboard shades each key by how many mistakes you made on it — the darker the red, the more errors. Use it to pick which keys to drill in your next few tests.

How can I type faster?

Keep your fingers on the home row, put accuracy before speed, practice in short daily sessions, and use the heatmap to drill your weakest keys. Our full guide to typing faster covers each step.

What is the home row on a keyboard?

The home row is the middle row of letter keys where your fingers rest when you touch type: A S D F for the left hand and J K L ; for the right. Your index fingers sit on F and J, which usually have small raised bumps so you can find them without looking.

What does the Adj. score mean?

Adj. score is your WPM multiplied by your accuracy percentage, so a fast but sloppy run scores lower than a slightly slower, cleaner one. For example, 60 WPM at 90% accuracy gives an adjusted score of 54.

Are there keyboard shortcuts?

Yes. Press Space or Enter to start typing, Esc to reset the test, and R to restart after a test or whenever the typing box isn't focused. When you're not typing, the number keys 1-6 switch between the 15, 30, 60, 120, 180, and 300-second tests.

What does the consistency score mean?

Consistency measures how steady your typing pace was, based on how much your speed varied from second to second. 100% would be perfectly even; long bursts followed by stalls pull it down. A steadier rhythm usually means fewer errors and a higher sustainable WPM.

Can I share my result?

Yes. After a test, use Copy result to copy your score as text, or Save image to download a shareable result image.

Is my typing data saved anywhere?

Your passage and keystrokes exist only for the duration of the test — refresh the page and they're gone. The exception is a few small things saved locally in your browser: your personal best WPM for each test length, your last 30 results, and a daily practice streak. None of it leaves your device. See the Privacy Policy for the full explanation.

How do I practice my weak keys?

After a test with mistakes, the results screen shows a keyboard heatmap and a Drill my weak keys button. It starts a test made of words that use your three most-missed letters. Drill sessions are not counted toward your personal bests or recent results, so they never lower your stats.

Can I make the text bigger?

Yes. Use the three A buttons next to the test controls to switch between small, medium, and large text. Your choice is remembered in your browser. The skip link and full keyboard navigation are also supported.

Where can I see my progress over time?

Under the test, the Your progress panel shows how many tests you have taken, your average and best WPM, your average accuracy, and a trend line from your last 30 results. It is stored only in your browser, and the Clear my data button removes it.
