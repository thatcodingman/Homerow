A 15-minute daily typing practice routine

Short, consistent sessions that build speed

Typing speed is a motor skill, so it improves the way any physical skill does: through frequent, focused repetition rather than rare marathon sessions. Fifteen minutes a day beats two hours once a week. Here is a routine you can run on Home Row with no setup.

The 15-minute routine

  • Minutes 0–3, warm up: one 30-second test in Words mode at a relaxed pace. Fingers on the home row, eyes on the text. Ignore the score.
  • Minutes 3–8, accuracy block: three 30-second tests at a deliberately slower pace, aiming for 97% accuracy or better. Check the heatmap after each one.
  • Minutes 8–12, speed push: two 60-second tests, nudging your pace up just past comfortable. Accept a few more errors here.
  • Minutes 12–15, weak spots: one Punctuation-mode test if symbols slow you down, otherwise another Words test. Note your worst two keys for tomorrow.

Why this order works

Starting slow lets your hands settle into correct finger positions before you ask them to go fast. The accuracy block builds clean muscle memory, and the speed push then stretches it. Ending with your weak keys means the last thing you practice is the thing you most need, which helps it stick.

Track progress the right way

One test tells you very little, because scores swing with the text, the mode, and how warmed up you are. Look at your average over a week instead. The "Your progress" panel under the test shows your average WPM, best WPM, and accuracy from your last 30 tests, all stored only in your browser. If the average creeps up while accuracy holds steady, the routine is working.

Keep the habit going

  • Practice at the same time each day, such as right before you start work
  • Watch your daily streak counter; not breaking the chain is surprisingly motivating
  • Stop a session early if accuracy collapses; tired practice reinforces mistakes
  • Expect plateaus. Progress tends to come in steps, with flat stretches in between

When to change the routine

Once accuracy sits comfortably above 97%, spend more of the session on the speed push. If you plateau for several weeks, try the longer 2-minute test to build stamina, or work on typing without looking if you still glance at the keys. For more habits, see how to type faster.
