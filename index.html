About the Home Row typing test

Home Row measures your typing speed in words per minute (WPM) along with accuracy and consistency, using the same formula touch-typing tests have used for decades: every 5 correctly typed characters counts as one word. Pick a duration from 15 seconds to 5 minutes, choose Words, Quotes, or Punctuation mode, and start typing — your pace updates live as you go.

Prefer a set length? Try the 30 second, 1 minute, 2 minute, 3 minute, or 5 minute typing test.

What counts as a good typing speed?

  • Under 30 WPM — beginner, still building muscle memory for key positions
  • 35–45 WPM — average for most adults doing everyday typing
  • 50–65 WPM — solid, comfortable touch-typing speed
  • 65–90 WPM — fast, well above the office-work average
  • 100+ WPM — professional transcriptionist or competitive-typist territory

Tips to improve your WPM

  • Keep your fingers on the home row (ASDF / JKL;) between keystrokes instead of hunting for each key
  • Slow down slightly and prioritize accuracy first — fixing mistakes costs more time than typing carefully
  • Practice in short, frequent bursts (a few 30-60s tests a day) rather than one long session
  • Use the heatmap on your results screen to see which keys you slip on most, and drill those specifically
  • Try Punctuation mode once Words mode feels easy — it mirrors real writing more closely

Frequently asked questions

How is WPM calculated?

WPM is the number of correctly typed characters divided by 5, divided by the time elapsed in minutes. Your raw WPM (shown alongside it) counts every keystroke before accuracy is factored in — see the full FAQ for more.

What is a good typing speed?

Most adults type 35-45 WPM; 65-90 WPM is fast, and 100+ WPM is professional-level. See the benchmarks above for the full breakdown.

Is my typing data saved or uploaded anywhere?

No — the test runs entirely in your browser. Only a few small things (your personal best per test length, your last few results, and a daily streak) are saved locally on your device. Details are in the Privacy Policy.

What is the difference between WPM and raw WPM?

Net WPM (what most people mean by "WPM") only counts correct words and is reduced by errors. Raw WPM counts everything you typed, so it's always equal to or higher than net WPM.
