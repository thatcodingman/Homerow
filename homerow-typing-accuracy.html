How to improve typing accuracy

Fewer errors, faster results

Accuracy is the quickest route to a higher typing speed. Every mistake costs a backspace and breaks your rhythm, so cutting errors usually raises your WPM without any extra effort. Here is how to type more cleanly. Curious where you stand now? Take the typing test and check the accuracy number.

Why accuracy drives speed

Look at the gap between your WPM and your raw WPM on the results screen. Raw WPM counts everything you typed, mistakes included, while WPM only counts what was correct. A big gap means errors are eating your score. Closing that gap is often worth more than trying to type faster.

Slow down to speed up

Drop your pace until you can finish a full test with almost no errors, then raise it gradually. Rushing builds sloppy habits, and sloppy habits are hard to unlearn. Clean repetitions at a comfortable speed build the muscle memory that later lets you go fast without mistakes.

Use the heatmap to find your problem keys

The "Where you slipped" keyboard on the results screen shades each key by how many errors you made on it. Note the two or three darkest keys and spend a few minutes typing slowly through words that use them. Targeted drilling beats general practice.

Fix the finger, not just the key

Many errors come from using the wrong finger or reaching from the wrong position. Hitting a neighboring key by mistake usually means your hand has drifted off the home row. Return to A S D F and J K L ; between reaches, and let each finger handle its own column of keys.

Watch for swapped letters and rushed word endings

Two of the most common slips are transposed letters (typing "teh" for "the") and hitting space too early at the end of a word. Both come from your hands getting ahead of your eyes. Slow down slightly on common short words and finish each word before moving on.

Keep your eyes ahead

Read a word or two ahead of what you are typing, and keep your eyes on the text instead of the keyboard. Looking down makes you lose your place and invites errors. If you must correct a typo, note that fixing it does not hurt your score in Home Row, but it still costs you time.

Manage fatigue

Accuracy drops when you are tired or tense. Keep your wrists neutral, shoulders relaxed, and stop practicing when your error count starts creeping up. Short sessions done fresh beat long ones done tired.

A simple 5-minute accuracy drill

  • Take three 30-second tests in Words mode at a deliberately slower pace, aiming for 97% accuracy or better
  • Check the heatmap and note your worst two keys
  • Take one 60-second test in Punctuation mode to practice Shift and punctuation reaches
  • Repeat daily and gently raise your pace once accuracy stays high
